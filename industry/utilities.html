Utilities, power and water

Utilities run safety-critical networks under close regulatory oversight, often with prices and service levels set externally. Compliance is not discretionary, and neither is asset safety.

What usually goes wrong here

  • A gap between what is delivered into the network and what is billed, filled with estimates
  • Licence and determination conditions nobody owns, so compliance is asserted rather than evidenced
  • Safety-critical maintenance deferred under cost pressure, with the risk transferred to the public
  • Customers in hardship or dependent on supply for health reasons, treated as ordinary arrears cases
  • Capital projects capitalised before the asset is actually in service

Compliance areas to check locally

Expect obligations covering the operating licence and price controls, customer protection and vulnerability rules, network safety and environmental standards, metering accuracy, and regulatory reporting. The specific law, regulator and reporting standard differ by country.
